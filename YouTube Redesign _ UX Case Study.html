1. Executive summary and scorecard

This project redesigns the YouTube landing and watch pages around purpose. On opening, the viewer picks a mode: Student/Professional, Kids or Entertainment. Each mode has its own colour theme, a filtered home feed and search behaviour, and Kids mode is protected by a parent PIN that can be created, entered or reset. A light/dark toggle and a mode legend in the header sit on top of a simplified left rail and filter chips.

Compared with the first version I reviewed (a mustard header pill and olive buttons on cream, with the red logo), the concept is now much stronger: the idea is clearer, the themes are distinct, the desktop layout has moved to a calmer cream-and-sage look, and the Kids PIN flow is designed.

The main risks are legal and accessibility-related: several thumbnails and video frames use third-party artwork and real people (a film poster, a children's cartoon and a comedy character, and face-heavy shorts), and the sage buttons with white text sample at about 3.7:1 contrast, below the 4.5:1 needed for small text.

1.1 What has improved

  • The concept: purpose-based modes are a memorable, explainable idea that solves a real problem (one feed for very different needs).

  • Themes: Student (cream and sage), Kids (warm browns and beige) and Entertainment (navy) are instantly distinguishable.

  • Kids safety flow: Enter Pin, Create New Pin (email, new pin, confirm) and Forgotten Pin screens show responsible design.

  • Mode-aware search: 'biology' in Student mode, 'cocomelon' in Kids and 'aquaman' in Entertainment show that results follow the mode.

  • Cleaner desktop layout: simplified left rail, filter chips (All, Podcast, Music, Channel, Series, Movies, More), Shorts row and a light/dark toggle.

3. Problem, solution and benefit

3.1 Problem

A single YouTube feed is asked to serve very different needs: a student who wants focused, educational content, a young child who needs a safe environment, and an adult who wants entertainment. The result is distraction for students, worry for parents and generic recommendations for everyone.

Children are heavy users: Ofcom reported that 89% of UK children aged 3 to 17 used YouTube in 2021, making it their most-used platform. Parents need clear, understandable controls, and adults need a calm, relevant start page.

3.2 Solution

Ask the viewer why they are here. A splash screen with a mode toggle leads to a mode dialog (Student, Kids or Entertainment). Each mode applies a theme, filters the home feed and search, and changes the tone of the interface. Kids mode asks a parent to create a PIN (email, new PIN, confirm) and requires it again to leave. The header keeps a small legend of the three modes and a light/dark toggle, and the left rail is simplified.

3.3 Benefit

  • Focus for students: less entertainment noise and a calm theme.

  • Confidence for parents: a clear PIN-protected Kids mode with recovery.

  • Relevance for everyone: search and recommendations follow the chosen purpose.

  • Brand differentiation: warmer, calmer identity than the default red, white and black.

  • Inclusive options: light and dark themes, clearer chips, and a simpler rail.

6. User stories and acceptance criteria

ID

User story

Acceptance criteria (Given / When / Then)

US-01

As a viewer, I want to choose a mode at the start so that the page fits my purpose.

- Given I open the site, then I see a mode toggle and a dialog offering Student, Kids and Entertainment.

- Given I choose a mode, then the theme and home feed change accordingly.

US-02

As a student, I want a calm, focused feed so that I stay on task.

- Given Student mode, when I search 'biology', then results prioritise educational content.

- Given Student mode, then the theme is cream and sage with no autoplay previews.

US-03

As a parent, I want Kids mode protected by a PIN so that my child cannot leave it.

- Given Kids mode is first selected, when I enter my email, a new PIN and confirmation, then a PIN is created.

- Given Kids mode is active, when someone tries to change mode, then the PIN is required.

US-04

As a parent, I want to recover a forgotten PIN so that I am never locked out.

- Given I choose 'Forgotten PIN', then a reset link is sent to the recovery email.

- Given I follow the link and set a new PIN, then I can enter Kids mode.

US-05

As an entertainment viewer, I want a dark, cinematic mode with filters for Series and Movies so that I can relax.

- Given Entertainment mode, then the theme is navy and the filter chips include Series and Movies.

US-06

As a viewer, I want a clear header showing my current mode and a light/dark toggle so that I always know where I am.

- Given any page, then the header shows the active mode as text and the toggle state.

- Given I toggle light or dark, then all pages change theme together.

US-07

As a viewer, I want search to follow my mode so that results are relevant and safe.

- Given Kids mode, then results contain only content marked suitable for children.

- Given Entertainment mode, then results may include films and series.

US-08

As a viewer with access needs, I want captions, autoplay control and scalable text so that I can watch comfortably.

- Given a video, then captions can be turned on and off.

- Given autoplay or hover previews, then I can disable them.

- Given text is set to 200%, then no content is cut off.

11. Technical documentation

11.1 Architecture and recommended stack

A web front end (React) renders the mode-specific theme from design tokens. A feed service returns videos filtered by the active mode's content rules using audience and category metadata set by moderation. A search service (OpenSearch or similar) applies the same filter. A PIN service stores a hashed PIN per Kids profile with a recovery email flow and rate limiting. Watch history is stored per user and per mode.

11.4 Software development life cycle (SDLC) recommendation

Recommended model: Dual-track Agile: Design Thinking and Lean UX discovery running alongside Scrum delivery, with child-safety and accessibility review gates.

Why this model fits

  • The biggest unknown is whether people want modes, which is a discovery question best answered with prototypes and tests before heavy engineering.

  • Lean UX (build, measure, learn) fits an experiment such as 'does a mode switch reduce time to relevant video'.

  • Scrum gives delivery rhythm once a mode is validated.

  • Kids mode has legal and safety requirements, so formal review gates (moderation, privacy, accessibility) sit at the end of each iteration.

  • Feature flags allow mode-by-mode rollout and A/B tests.

12. Design system

12.1 Colour theory

Each mode uses colour to set a mood. Student mode pairs warm cream (#FCF6E0, #F6EEE2) with sage green (#5C9170, #4A7B59): green suggests growth and calm focus, and cream reduces glare. Entertainment mode uses deep navy (#26293A, #1A1C29) with warm orange (#F1A55A), a classic complementary blue and orange pairing that feels cinematic. Kids mode uses warm browns (#392D1E, #694A35) with beige (#E9DBC8) and orange accents for warmth and comfort.

The mood colours change, but the layout does not. This is the key structural idea: theme signals purpose while layout stays familiar, so users never have to relearn the interface.

Because colour alone must not tell users which mode they are in, the header carries a text legend and the active mode is named in the dialog and page.

Kids mode deserves a second look: dark brown gives a serious, heavy tone. Keep the warm hue family but raise lightness and saturation of accents so it feels playful, while keeping 4.5:1 text contrast.

12.5 Styling strategies

  • Mode-driven design tokens: one component library, three token sets for colour, with shared spacing and type.

  • Purpose-first onboarding: the first decision shapes everything after it.

  • Reduce noise: fewer colours per screen, simplified left rail, fewer competing thumbnails.

  • Consistent layout across modes so that only mood changes.

  • Safety by design: PIN, recovery email and re-verification on exit from Kids mode.

  • Inclusive defaults: light and dark themes, captions, autoplay control, larger targets.

12.6 Design structure and layout

  • Header: logo, search, time, mode legend, Create post, light/dark toggle.

  • Left rail: Home, Shorts, Create post, Subscriptions, Games, Your channel, Your videos, Playlist, Liked, Watch later, History, with 'Show more'.

  • Main area: filter chips, video row, Shorts row.

  • Watch page: large player, title, channel and subscribe, description, and a right rail of related videos.

12.7 Components, iconography and imagery

  • Icons: line icons with text labels in the rail.

  • Imagery: use original illustrations or licensed and credited photos and video frames.

  • Buttons and chips: filled deep sage or orange with dark text; outlined for secondary.

  • Dialogs: centred card with mode options and clear labels.


13. WCAG 2.2 accessibility audit

Short answer: not yet compliant, and a concept cannot be certified. For a video product the WCAG criteria that matter most are captions, autoplay and motion control, contrast, and accessible authentication for the Kids PIN.

Likely failures are white text on the lighter sage buttons and sage text on cream. The PIN needs an alternative to memorising (WCAG 3.3.8).

Target: WCAG 2.2 Level AA (the current W3C Recommendation since 5 October 2023). Statuses: Likely pass, Needs test, Likely fail (fix), Not verifiable in a static design.

14. Visual appeal evaluation and fix list

Overall visual appeal: 7 out of 10

The idea is the strongest part: it is easy to explain and instantly shows a designer's view of the problem. The cream and sage desktop version is polished and welcoming, and the three themes show range.

The design is held back by heavy use of third-party imagery, small metadata text, a Kids theme that feels dark, and sage-on-white contrast.

What works

  • Clear, memorable concept

  • Distinct mode themes with a consistent layout

  • Responsible Kids PIN flow with recovery

  • Simplified navigation and chips

15. Results, next steps and interview talking points

Overall result

  • A distinctive product concept with a clear rationale and three coherent themes.

  • A complete parental-control flow (create, enter, forgot).

  • Documentation that ties the design to data models, safety and accessibility.

Next steps

  • Replace third-party imagery and add the concept disclaimer.

  • Fix contrast tokens.

  • Interview 6 to 8 people (students, parents, casual viewers) and test the mode dialog.

  • Add player states (captions, autoplay off) and Kids exit screens.

  • 'I treated Kids mode as a safety feature, with PIN recovery and re-verification.'
